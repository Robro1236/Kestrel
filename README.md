# Kestrel

**Open the app here: https://kestrel-tan.vercel.app**

It's free. You don't need an account and you don't install anything.

---

## What is Kestrel?

Kestrel helps a family **get ready for a disaster** (a flood, fire, earthquake, storm, heat wave, power outage and more) **before it happens**.

You tell it two things:

1. **Where you live**
2. **Who lives with you** (adults, seniors, children, babies, pets)

In about a minute, it makes **your household's own emergency plan**:

- a **shopping and packing list** with real amounts, such as how many gallons of water *your* family needs;
- what **disasters are most likely where you live**;
- what to **do right now** if something is happening;
- where the **nearest hospital, fire station, police, pharmacy and shelter** are;
- whether your **water is safe**, and how to make it safe;
- a **family plan card** with everyone's phone numbers, medical needs and meeting places, which you can print or email.

Kestrel does not make up any safety advice. Every fact comes from a trusted public source (FEMA, the National Weather Service, the EPA, Ready.gov and others), and the app shows which one.

## Please open it on your PHONE

> **Use your phone. Kestrel is made for phones first.**
>
> It works on a computer too, but a phone is much better:
> - In an emergency, your phone is what you'll have in your hand, not a laptop.
> - **Use my location** finds where you are by itself.
> - **Directions** open your phone's maps app.
> - Phone numbers can be tapped to call.
> - **Email the card** attaches your printable plan as a file.
> - You can put Kestrel on your home screen like a normal app.

### Start Guide

1. Pick up your phone and unlock it.
2. Open your web browser:
   - **iPhone:** tap **Safari** (the blue compass icon).
   - **Android:** tap **Chrome** (the red, yellow, green and blue circle).
3. Tap the **address bar** at the top or bottom of the screen (the long box where web addresses go).
4. Type exactly: **kestrel-tan.vercel.app**
5. Tap **Go** (or the arrow / Enter key) on the keyboard.
6. Wait a second or two. You'll see the word **Kestrel** at the top and a big button that says **Make my plan**. You're in!

**Want a fresh start, like a brand-new user?** Type this link instead:
**kestrel-tan.vercel.app/?start**
It clears any old answers on that phone and starts from the beginning. 

### On a computer

Open any browser (Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox), click the address bar, type **kestrel-tan.vercel.app** and press **Enter**. Everything works, but we still recommend a phone.

## How to make your plan (step by step)

### Step 1: The welcome screen

You'll see:

- **"Be ready for a disaster."** at the top.
- A big blue button: **Make my plan**. Underneath it says *"Free, about a minute, no account."*
- **"N households prepared"**: how many households have made a plan with Kestrel.
- **Share with a neighbor**: sends the Kestrel link to someone else. On a phone it opens your share menu (Messages, WhatsApp, email…). On a computer it copies the link so you can paste it.
- Three cards that explain what you'll get: *A kit sized to your home*, *What could hit your area*, and *Ready when the network is down*.
- **View my plan** shows up only if you already made a plan on this device. Tap it to go straight back to your plan.

- **Tap "Make my plan".**

### Step 2: "Where are you?"

Choose **one** of these:

- **Easiest:** tap **Use my location**. Your phone will ask *"Allow Kestrel to use your location?"* Tap **Allow**. Kestrel finds your town by itself.
- **Or type it:** tap the box that says *"or type a US ZIP code or a city"*, and type your ZIP code (like **95376**) or your city (like **Tracy, CA**). A list of matches pops up underneath. **Tap the right one** to be sure Kestrel picks the correct place. (If you don't tap one, Kestrel uses its best match for what you typed.)

### Step 3: "Who is in your household?"

Each row has a **−** (minus) and a **+** (plus) button. Tap **+** to add one, **−** to take one away. Count yourself too!

| Row | Who it means |
|---|---|
| **Adults** | People aged 18 to 64 |
| **Seniors** | People aged 65 and older |
| **Children** | Kids aged 2 to 17 |
| **Infants** | Babies under 2 |
| **Dogs** | Your dogs |
| **Cats** | Your cats |
| **Other pets** | Birds, rabbits, and other animals |

Below that are two switches. Tap a switch to turn it **on** (it turns colored) if it's true for your home:

- **Someone takes daily medication**: adds medicine items to your list.
- **Someone uses a mobility aid or powered medical device**: (a wheelchair, walker, oxygen machine, CPAP and so on) adds backup power and mobility items.

**Why does this matter?** The amounts change with your family. Four people need more water than one. A baby needs formula and diapers. A dog needs dog food. Kestrel only shows you what *your* home needs.

- **Tap "Build my kit".**

(**About Kestrel** at the bottom of this screen takes you back to the welcome screen, which explains what Kestrel does.)

### Step 4: Your plan

This is the main screen. From top to bottom:

1. **Your town's name** and **"Your emergency plan"**.
2. **"X of N ready"** and a progress bar: how much of your kit you already have. When you scroll down, a thin progress bar stays stuck at the top so you can always see it.
3. Two small buttons:
   - **Edit household**: go back and change where you live or who lives with you. Your list updates by itself.
   - **Family plan card**: opens your family card (see *Family plan card* below).
4. **"Something happening now?"**: tap it if a disaster is happening **right now**. If there's an active weather warning where you live, this card turns into **"<the warning>: what to do now"** instead.
5. **Weather warnings** from the National Weather Service:
   - **"All clear"** means there are no warnings for your area right now.
   - If there is a warning, it's shown here, with **Read the full warnings on weather.gov**.
   - Outside the US it says *"Live warnings are only available in the US for now."*
6. Some cards only appear **when there is a problem**, so if you don't see them, that's good news:
   - **Air quality**: when the air is unhealthy (smoke, smog).
   - **Water safety / Your water system**: when there's a problem with your water.
   - **County help**: when your county has active alerts.
7. **SUPPLY KIT**: your packing and shopping list (explained next).
8. **Extra advice**: helpful tips (explained below).

## The Supply Kit (your checklist)

The kit is split into groups:

- **Water and food**
- **Light and power**
- **Health and safety**
- **Papers and money**
- **For your household** (things for your babies, seniors, pets and medical needs)
- **For your area's hazards** (things for the disasters most likely where you live, like an earthquake or flood)

**How to use it:**

- **Tap the box** next to an item when you **have it** (or after you buy it). A check mark appears and your **"X of N ready"** goes up.
- Each item shows **how much** you need (for example, *"12 gallons"*), **why** you need it, and **where that advice comes from** (*"Source: …"*).
- **Tap the ⓘ button** (it says "More about …") next to an item to read more details about it.
- **Tap a group's title** to fold it shut or open it again. When every item in a group is checked, the group celebrates 🎉 and folds itself up.
- At the bottom: *"Sized for your area's hazards: FEMA National Risk Index, fetched DATE."* This tells you where the hazard data came from and when.

Your check marks are saved on your phone. Close the app, come back tomorrow, and they're still there.

### What is "Extra advice" for?

**Extra advice** is the list of tips at the bottom of your plan. These are **not things to pack**, so they have **no checkbox and no amount**. They are things to **do** or **know** that make your family safer. Like the kit, they change with your household and your area. Examples:

- **Children:** pack a small comfort item (a toy or blanket), and make a child ID card.
- **Pets:** keep a photo of your pet with you, in case you get separated.
- **Earthquakes:** strap tall furniture to the wall, and learn where your gas shutoff is.
- **Floods:** know your way out to high ground, and think about flood insurance.
- **Heat:** cooling towels, and check on neighbors (especially older ones).
- **Wildfire:** keep a bag ready by the door, and consider an air purifier for smoke.
- **Hurricanes:** shutters for your windows, and fill up on fuel early.
- **Tornadoes:** know your safe room.
- **Winter storms:** hand warmers, and salt for icy steps.
- **Babies:** diaper rash cream.

## The menu (☰)

Tap **☰** (three lines, top right of every screen) to open the menu. Tap any item to go there. The page you're already on can't be tapped, because you're already there.

Tapping the **Kestrel** logo at the top left always takes you back to your plan.

| Menu item | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Home** | Your plan (the main screen). |
| **What's happening?** | For a disaster happening **right now**. See below. |
| **Local hazards** | Which disasters are most likely where you live. |
| **Water** | How much water to store, and how to make water safe to drink. |
| **Nearby help** | Hospitals, fire stations, police, pharmacies, food banks and shelters near you. |
| **After a disaster** | How to stay safe once it's over. |
| **County alerts** | Local links for **San Joaquin County, CA** only (it only shows up there). |
| **Disaster history** | Past federal disasters in your county. |
| **Family plan card** | Your family's contacts, medical info and meeting places. |
| **Settings → Reset this device** | Erases everything Kestrel saved on this phone. |

At the bottom of the menu: *"Everything is saved on this device only. Data from FEMA, NWS, EPA, Open-Meteo and Ready.gov."*

### What's happening?

Use this **during** an emergency.

1. Tap what's happening: **Told to evacuate**, **Tornado**, **Hurricane**, **Flood**, **Wildfire or smoke**, **Extreme heat**, **Winter storm**, **Earthquake** or **Power outage**.
2. You get:
   - **Numbered steps**: exactly what to do, in order, from Ready.gov.
   - **Grab from your kit**: the items from *your* kit you need now, marked as packed or not packed.
   - **Who to call**: 911, plus the nearest hospital and fire station.
   - **All nearby help**: a link to the full list of places near you.

**In a real emergency, always call 911 first.**

### Local hazards

Shows the **top risk** for your county in one sentence, then a list of hazards with how risky each one is (from the FEMA National Risk Index). **Tap a hazard** to see what to do about it, plus **Read more on Ready.gov**. (US only.)

### Water

- **Water safety:** how many gallons to store for your household, water that's already in your home (like a water heater), and **If you need to make water safe** (boiling and bleach steps). Outside the US it tells you whether tap water is safe in your country.
- **Your water system** (US): the EPA record for the water company that serves you. *"No violations reported in the last 3 years"* is good news. If there were problems, they're listed with a link to the **Full EPA report for this system**.

### Nearby help

A list of the closest **hospitals, fire stations, police, pharmacies, food banks and shelters**, with how far away each one is (miles in the US, km elsewhere).

- Tap **Directions** to open your maps app.
- Tap a **phone number** to call it.
- *"In an emergency call 911. These are office lines."* Those numbers are for the front desk, not emergencies.
- **"Not an emergency room."** is shown under medical offices that aren't real ERs, so you don't drive to the wrong place.

### After a disaster

Safety steps for after it's over, such as: run generators **outdoors only**, at least 20 feet from doors and windows (carbon monoxide can kill); photograph damage before you clean up; which food to throw away after a power outage; and when tap water is safe again.

### County alerts (San Joaquin County only)

- **Sign up for SJReady alerts**: get official emergency texts from the county.
- **Know your evacuation zone**: a map of zones and how they work, so you know when it's your turn to leave.
- **Cooling centers**: cool places to go during a heat wave.

### Disaster history

Every **federal disaster declaration** for your county (for example, 25 for San Joaquin County), with the date and the type. It shows what has *really* happened where you live.

### Family plan card

Fill it in **together as a family**, then print it or share it so everyone has a copy.

- **Top:** Emergency **911** and **Poison Help 1-800-222-1222** (US, 24 hours).
- **Made for your household:** shows who's in your home, with **Edit household** to change it.
- **Each person:** one page per person. Tap a name at the top to switch people. For each person, fill in:
  - **Name**
  - **Phone**
  - **Medical needs**: allergies, medicines and doses, conditions, medical devices (for babies: formula, food and medicine).
  - **Doctors and pharmacy**: name and phone.
  - **Health insurance**: company, policy number and phone.
  - **School and work** (or childcare for babies): the phone number, and where they take people in an emergency.
  - **Photo**: tap **Add photo** to add a recent picture, in case you get separated.
  - Tap **Next: (name)** to move to the next person.
- **Where to meet:**
  - **Meeting place near home**: for example the mailbox at the corner (for a fire at home).
  - **Meeting place outside the neighborhood**: for example a library or a friend's house (if you can't get home).
- **Who to call:** an **out-of-area contact**, someone in another town everyone checks in with. Calls to far-away numbers often get through when local lines are busy.
- **Pets** (if you have any): names, microchip numbers, the vet's phone, a pet-friendly place to stay, and **Add photo** (a photo of you *with* your pet helps prove it's yours).
- **Home:** where your water valve, gas meter and electric panel are; your home or renters insurance; and your evacuation zone.
- **Buttons at the bottom:**
  - **Print**: prints everyone on one card. Put a copy on the fridge, in your car and in your kit.
  - **Email the card**: on a phone, opens your share menu with the printable card attached as a file (`family-emergency-plan.html`). Pick Mail, type an address, and send. On a computer, it opens your email app with the plan written in the email.
  - **Back to your plan**: returns to the main screen.

Everything you type is saved **only on your device**. Nobody else can see it.

### Settings → Reset this device

Tap **Reset this device**, then tap again when it says **"Tap again to erase"**. This deletes your household, location, checklist and family card from this phone. Use it if you're giving your phone away or want to start completely over. **It can't be undone.**

## Good things to know

- **Works without internet.** After you open Kestrel once, your plan, kit and family card still open with no signal, which is exactly when you might need them. The plan shows the date it was saved.
- **Private.** No account, no sign-up, no name or email asked for.
  - **Stays on your phone only:** who lives with you, your checklist, and everything on your family plan card (names, phones, medical info, photos).
  - **Sent to our server:** your location (or the ZIP code or city you typed), only so Kestrel can look up the hazards, weather warnings, water system and nearby places for that spot. Nothing else about you (no name, no household, no account) is sent with it.
  - **Counted:** making a plan adds 1 to the "households prepared" number. Only the number goes up; nothing about you is sent with it.
- **Where it works:** the kit, water advice and nearby help work **anywhere in the world**. Local hazards, weather warnings and the water system record are **US only**.
- **Sources:** FEMA National Risk Index, National Weather Service, EPA drinking-water records, CMS hospital list, Ready.gov, OpenStreetMap and Open-Meteo.

## If something goes wrong

| What you see | What to do |
|---|---|
| *"We couldn't get your location. Type a ZIP code or a city name instead."* | You tapped **Don't Allow**, or your phone's location is off. Just type your ZIP code or city in the box instead. (To use location later, turn on Location for your browser in your phone's settings.) |
| *"Add at least one person to your household."* | Tap **+** next to at least one row (count yourself!), then tap **Build my kit** again. |
| *"We couldn't find that place. Try a ZIP code or a city name."* | Check the spelling, or try your ZIP code instead. |
| *"Couldn't reach the location service. Check your connection and try again."* | You have no internet. Connect to Wi-Fi or turn on mobile data, then try again. |
| *"Couldn't load your area's hazards, so this is the basic kit."* | The hazard data didn't load (usually a weak connection). Your basic kit is still right. Come back later and the hazard items will be added. |
| *"Couldn't load nearby places right now."* | Weak or no connection. If you looked before, Kestrel shows the list it saved last time, with that date. |
| *"Not found within 6 miles"* (or *10 km*) | There's no place of that type close to you. It isn't an error. |
| *"…some places may be missing."* under Nearby help | The main map source was down, so Kestrel used a backup that finds fewer places. Check again later. |
| *"Couldn't load your county…"* or *"Couldn't load FEMA's disaster history right now…"* | No connection, or the government site is slow. Try again in a minute. |
| *"Couldn't add that photo. Try another one."* | Pick a different photo (a normal camera photo works best). |
| The app won't open with **no internet** | Offline only works **after you've opened Kestrel once with internet**. Open it once while connected and it will work offline after that. |
| You want to change where you live or who lives with you | On your plan, tap **Edit household**. |
| A checkbox was tapped by mistake | Tap it again to uncheck it. |
| You want to start over completely | **☰** → **Reset this device** → tap again to confirm. Or open **kestrel-tan.vercel.app/?start**. |
| Something still looks wrong | Close the browser tab, open the link again, and pull down on the page to refresh. |

## Why Kestrel

**The problem.** In FEMA's 2023 National Household Survey (over 7,600 adults), only 51% said they were prepared for a disaster. The usual apps hand everyone the same checklist: no quantities, nothing about where you live, nothing about water, which is one of Rotary's focus areas and the first thing people run out of.

**What's different.** Kestrel builds the plan from two answers, where you live and who lives with you:

- The kit has real amounts (gallons of water for *this* household), and infant, senior and pet items appear only when they apply.
- Your county's FEMA risks add items and lead with the top risk. When something is happening, one screen shows what to do, what to grab from your own kit, and the nearest emergency room.
- The family plan card gives each person their own page (phone, medical needs, doctors, insurance, school or work) and prints as one card.
- "Share with a neighbor" passes it on, and the home screen counts households prepared.

**Trust.** Kestrel writes no safety advice of its own. Every fact names its public source and date.

**How it's built.**

- One Expo (React Native + TypeScript) codebase: the web app judges open today, and the same code builds for iOS and Android when Rotary publishes it.
- A Supabase edge function caches public data so it stays inside free tiers.
- A service worker keeps the app working offline.
- Automated tests, a bundle-size budget, and load and smoke checks (see [CONSTRAINTS.md](CONSTRAINTS.md)).

**What's next.** Store release through Rotary, county links beyond San Joaquin, and more hazard data outside the US.

Demo walkthrough: [docs/DEMO.md](docs/DEMO.md).

Built for the Rotary Club HackAIthon ("Create Lasting Impact"). Expo (React Native + TypeScript) on the web, with a Supabase backend.
